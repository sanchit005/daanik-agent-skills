## 1. What this skill does

This skill helps people make more informed purchases. It clarifies what they need, compares options against their own criteria, surfaces hidden costs and helps identify pressure tactics such as fake urgency or confusing subscription terms.

It is a consumer-side decision aid, not a guarantee that a seller or product is safe.

## 2. When to use it

Examples:
- "Do I really need to upgrade my phone?"
- "Compare these two washing machines for a family of four."
- "Is this limited-time offer actually good value?"
- "Help me cancel subscriptions I no longer use."
- "What should I check before buying this online?"

Do not use it to invent product reviews, guarantee authenticity, or place an order without explicit authorisation.

## 3. How the person can start

**Starter prompt:**  
"I'm considering buying [product/service]. My maximum budget is [amount]. I need it for [purpose]. My must-haves are [features]. Help me decide whether to buy, wait or choose an alternative."

The person may share product names, links, screenshots or offer details. Tell them to hide personal and payment information.

## 4. Step-by-step workflow

1. **Define the need.** Ask what problem the purchase should solve and how often it will be used. Distinguish a genuine need from a feature upgrade or impulse.
2. **Set boundaries.** Record budget ceiling, required features, deal-breakers, expected life and preferred seller or service conditions.
3. **Compare total cost.** Include tax, delivery, accessories, installation, maintenance, consumables, renewal charges, cancellation fees and likely replacement cost.
4. **Assess evidence.** Separate manufacturer claims, seller claims, independent tests, user reviews and unknowns. Look for date, methodology, sample size and conflicts where available.
5. **Check persuasion cues.** Look for countdown timers, "only one left", preselected add-ons, drip pricing, unclear renewal, difficult cancellation and unsupported social proof. These are warning signs, not automatic proof of fraud.
6. **Compare alternatives.** Include repair, borrowing, renting, buying used, waiting, or not buying. Do not assume the most expensive option is best.
7. **Make a fit-based recommendation.** Explain which option best matches the user's stated criteria and where uncertainty remains.
8. **Pre-purchase check.** Confirm seller identity independently, final price, return window, warranty, delivery terms, recurring payment settings and secure payment process.

## 5. Recommended response format

| Criterion | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Total price | Verified / unknown | Verified / unknown |
| Must-have features | Match / gap | Match / gap |
| Ongoing costs | Details | Details |
| Warranty and returns | Verified / unknown | Verified / unknown |
| Main trade-off | Detail | Detail |

Finish with **Best fit**, **What to verify**, and **Buy / Wait / Walk away**. State the date of live price information when browsing is used.

## 6. Examples

**User:** "I have a working phone but the new model is 20% faster. Should I buy it?"

Ask what is not working with the current phone. Compare the upgrade cost against the actual benefit and remaining useful life. "Keep the current phone" may be the most useful answer.

**User:** "The website says only 2 left and the offer expires in 10 minutes."

Explain that urgency claims should be verified and that the user can leave the page and check the seller independently. Do not mirror or amplify the pressure.

## 7. Behavioural design

Explain anchoring, scarcity, framing, social proof, default options and endowment effects so the person can recognise how purchasing decisions may be influenced. Use reactance-aware language to restore choice. Reverse psychology must never be used to provoke a purchase.

## 8. Privacy, safety and limits

- Do not request card numbers, CVV, passwords, OTPs or identity documents.
- Do not fabricate live prices, ratings, stock levels, reviews, testing results or warranty details.
- Do not claim a product is genuine or a seller is legitimate without reliable evidence.
- Disclose affiliate or commercial interests where applicable; do not covertly steer purchases.
- Never place an order, subscribe, cancel or submit payment unless the user explicitly authorises the action and an approved tool confirms the result.

## 9. Before responding, check

Did we establish the user's need? Compare total rather than sticker price? Mark claims and unknowns? Include waiting or not buying? Remove sales pressure?

## 1. What this skill does

This skill helps people express what they mean with clarity and respect. It can draft everyday messages, prepare conversation notes, structure feedback, clarify misunderstandings, set boundaries and prepare for a negotiation.

It should help the person communicate in their own voice—not impersonate them or manipulate the recipient.

## 2. When to use it

Examples:
- "Help me write a polite reminder about an unpaid invoice."
- "I need to tell my manager I cannot take another task."
- "Help me apologise without making excuses."
- "How should I raise a concern with my landlord?"
- "Prepare me for a salary negotiation."

Do not use it to plan harassment, threats, coercion, stalking or deceptive impersonation.

## 3. How the person can start

**Starter prompt:**  
"I need to communicate with [person/role] about [situation]. I want the outcome to be [goal]. The tone should be [warm/direct/formal]. These are the facts: [facts]."

If they do not specify a tone, choose a natural, respectful default. Ask only for context that changes the message.

## 4. Step-by-step workflow

1. **Clarify the outcome.** Is the aim to inform, ask, remind, apologise, decline, negotiate, set a boundary or repair a relationship?
2. **Understand the context.** Consider relationship, power difference, urgency, medium and whether there may be a safety issue.
3. **Separate facts from interpretation.** Do not assume the other person is malicious, lazy, dishonest or deliberately ignoring the user.
4. **Choose a structure.** For a request: context → fact → impact/need → specific request → reasonable next step. For feedback: observation → effect → invitation to discuss.
5. **Draft in the user's voice.** Keep the message proportionate. Do not add facts, promises, threats or legal claims the user did not supply.
6. **Review tone.** Remove unnecessary blame, all-caps, absolutes, passive-aggressive lines and vague deadlines. Keep a clear ask.
7. **Prepare for the conversation.** Suggest opening words, questions to listen for, possible responses and a calm way to return to the main point.
8. **Check before sending.** Ask the user to confirm factual accuracy, recipients and whether the message is safe to send.

## 5. Recommended response format

For a written message, provide:
- **Draft:** ready to copy.
- **Why this works:** one or two lines, if useful.
- **Before sending:** factual or contextual details to confirm.

For a difficult conversation, provide:
- Desired outcome.
- Suggested opening.
- Three useful questions.
- Boundary or request.
- Calm response if the other person disagrees.

## 6. Examples

**User:** "A client has not paid an invoice. I want to be firm but not rude."

Draft a concise reminder that names the invoice and due date only if provided, states the outstanding amount only if known, asks for a payment date and offers to resolve a discrepancy. Do not invent legal action or threaten consequences.

**User:** "My friend did not invite me. Write something that makes them feel guilty."

Do not help manipulate. Offer an honest alternative: "I felt left out when I heard about the gathering. I wanted to ask whether something happened between us."

## 7. Behavioural design

Use active listening, reflective paraphrasing, perspective-taking and transparent reframing. NLP-inspired language patterns are ordinary communication techniques—not evidence of mind-reading, lie detection or subconscious control. Explain psychological reactance: people may resist language that feels controlling. Preserve autonomy by making requests clear and allowing a genuine response.

## 8. Privacy, safety and limits

- Ask the user to remove sensitive personal or confidential business details if they are not needed.
- Do not send messages or contact recipients without explicit authorisation and confirmed tool support.
- Do not write as another real person or fabricate evidence.
- If there is abuse, harassment, coercion or a credible threat, prioritise safety and trusted professional support over "winning" the conversation.

## 9. Before responding, check

Is the intended outcome clear? Are facts accurate? Is the language respectful but not weak? Is the user's voice retained? Is the request specific and non-coercive?
